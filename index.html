Research Focus

My current research develops the Survival-Breakdown Model (SBM), a foundational framework that reorders economic analysis around constitutive conditions, survivability, basic feasibility, and continuation, rather than treating optimization as the primitive starting point. The current three-paper series presents the integrated foundation of SBM, reclassifies demand-supply theory, general equilibrium theory, and macroeconomics as conditional special cases, and addresses major objections concerning its novelty, scope, and relation to standard theory.

The Survival-Breakdown Model: An Integrated Foundational Theory of Economic Agency

Author: Toyoharu Higuchi
Published: March 17, 2026
Repository: Zenodo
DOI: https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19020442

This paper presents the Survival-Breakdown Model (SBM) as an integrated foundational theory of economic agency. It argues that economic agents should be theorized not primarily from optimization, but from prior conditions of constitution, survivability, basic feasibility, and continuation.

The paper develops this perspective through five main steps: the distinction between survival and breakdown; the introduction of catastrophic shocks and resource decomposition; the definition of basic feasibility slack and feasible action sets; the dynamic structure of continuation, destabilization, and termination; and the distinction among constitutive goods, stabilizing goods, and choice goods.

It also argues that the principal contrast with standard utility theory lies not in representability itself, but in theoretical order. The order proposed here is: constitutive conditions, survivability, basic feasibility, continuation, and then optimization.

Demand-Supply Theory, General Equilibrium Theory, and Macroeconomics as Special Cases: A Reclassification of Mainstream Economics under the Survival-Breakdown Model

Author: Toyoharu Higuchi
Published: March 30, 2026
Repository: Zenodo
DOI: https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19312582

This paper argues that demand-supply theory, general equilibrium theory, and macroeconomics are not foundational first principles of economics, but lower-order and conditional special-case theories that acquire theoretical meaning only after prior conditions have already been secured.

On the basis of the Survival-Breakdown Model (SBM), the paper repositions demand and supply as a local form of market adjustment, general equilibrium as a special case of systemic adjustment, and macroeconomics as a field that should begin from continuation, destabilization, threshold proximity, and the possibility of cascading breakdown across households, firms, and institutions.

The paper does not deny the analytical usefulness of standard theories within relatively stable domains. Its claim is instead that their theoretical status must be changed: they are not universal starting points of economics, but lower-order theories supported by more prior conditions of constitution, survivability, basic feasibility, continuation, and sustainable participation.

The Survival-Breakdown Model and Standard Theory: A Clarification of the Main Criticisms and a Repositioning of Theoretical Order

Author: Toyoharu Higuchi
Published: March 31, 2026
Repository: Zenodo
DOI: https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19330520

This paper organizes the main criticisms that can be anticipated against the Survival-Breakdown Model (SBM), explains why many of them do not amount to decisive objections against its central thesis, and reconsiders the relation between SBM and standard economic theory from the standpoints of theoretical order, the conditions for the constitution of agents, domain, and scope of validity.

It argues that even if standard theory can formally describe phenomena akin to minimum survival, breakdown, inability to continue, and inability to participate, this does not show that optimization is the first principle of economics. The core issue is not representability itself, but what should come first in theory.

On that basis, the paper positions SBM not as a simple added constraint or a total replacement system, but as a theory of prior conditions that antecedently specifies the constitution of agents, survivability, basic feasibility, continuation, and sustainable participation, while standard theory is treated as a conditional and domain-limited lower-order theory.
