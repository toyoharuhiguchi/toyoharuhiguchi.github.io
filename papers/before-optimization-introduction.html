Before Optimization: A Short Introduction to the Survival-Breakdown Model

Toyoharu Higuchi
Published: April 2, 2026 · DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.19387403

This paper provides a short introduction to the Survival-Breakdown Model (SBM). Its central claim is that economic theory should not begin from optimization, equilibrium, or aggregate consistency taken by themselves, but from the prior conditions of constitution, survivability, basic feasibility, and continuation.

The paper presents this argument in compact form. It outlines the explanatory order proposed by SBM, provides a minimal formal sketch of survival, breakdown, basic feasibility, and continuation balance, and explains why demand-supply theory, general equilibrium theory, and macroeconomics should be repositioned as lower-order and conditionally valid theories rather than universal first principles.

This paper is intended as an entry point to the broader SBM program. It should be read together with the three existing core papers: the foundational paper, the reclassification paper, and the clarification paper.

Note on the English version: The authoritative version of this work is the original Japanese paper. The English version is provided as a reference translation for international readers and should be treated as supplementary to the Japanese original.
